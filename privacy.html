Privacy

Last updated: 29 October 2026

Nib Rally does not collect, store or share any personal data.

  • No accounts, no tracking, no ads. The app has no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party code that collects data.
  • Your progress stays on your device. Your ladder, the notes you learned, gold, settings and practice history (which days you practised, for how long, and how each match or practice went) are saved only on your device. Like any app's data, they can be part of your device's own backup to iCloud or Google, which only you can access. If you use "Copy save code", the code (one player's progress, name and Nib colour) is made on your device and goes only where you choose to share it.
  • Players and names. Up to four players can share one device, each with their own progress. A player's name is optional, is typed on the device and stays there. It is never sent to us.
  • Report for a teacher. "Share report for teacher" makes an image on your device with the player's name (if there is one), practice time and how well they read each note. Your device's share sheet sends it only where you choose, for example to a teacher. We never see it.
  • No internet needed. The game works offline. It does not send anything to us or anyone else.
  • Email. If you write to us, we use your address and message only to reply, and we never share them. We keep them only as long as we need them to help you, and we delete them whenever you ask. "Report a problem" in the game only prepares an email in your own email app. It adds the app version, device type, language and ladder position, the last error messages the game noted on your device (technical text such as "SyntaxError at line 1582", nothing personal), and your save code only if you tick the box. If the game ever cannot start, a "Something wrong?" screen offers the same kind of email. Nothing is sent unless you send it.
  • Children. Because nothing is collected, the app is suitable for children. The three things that can take a player's name or save out of the app ("Report a problem", "Copy save code" and "Share report for teacher") first ask a grown-up to answer a short sum.
